# XR communities and XR Guild: the in-person demo format

Format: carousel · 1080×1350
Goal: Spread awareness of XR communities by showing the format is established across cities

## Brand direction

Brand: XR Guild
Audience: The demo-night audience: enthusiasts, educators, and complete beginners who are curious about virtual and augmented reality and want to try it alongside other people. Deliberately broader than XR Guild's membership, which is working professionals across XR and spatial computing. Demo nights are open to anyone, free, with nothing to join. Members and non-members are equally expected at the door. They find XR Guild on Luma and LinkedIn and meet it in person at events held in bars and clubs.
Voice: open, warm, plain
Visual direction: Dark, roomy, and human. Cool indigo ground with one cyan signal, heavy geometric headlines, generous space, softened corners, and real photographs of people trying things. It should look like a good night in a dark room, not like a technology company.
Colors: Void #08070F — deepest ground, Indigo #181143 — primary brand ground, Panel #221A57 — raised panels, Cyan #5CE1F2 — accent, one use per surface, White #FFFFFF — headlines on dark, Silver #C3CADE — body text on dark, Muted #8E96B4 — captions only, Paper #F4F5F9 — documents and print
Typography: Headline: Space Grotesk Bold, tracking -0.02em, fallback Arial Black then Helvetica, Body: Figtree 400/500/700, fallback system sans, Labels and numbers: JetBrains Mono 400/500, tracking 0.08em, uppercase, fallback ui-monospace
Imagery: Use: photographs of real people at real events — mid-demo, being handed a headset, laughing. Faces and hands., Treatment: venue lighting as shot. Darken behind text rather than washing the image out. No heavy filters or grading toward the palette., Avoid: product shots with nobody holding the headset, all stock photography, generic 3D grids and purple neon metaverse abstractions, and anyone who did not agree to be photographed., Alternative when no photograph exists: the lens texture in brand/08-texture, as a background only.
Logo use: Master lockup is brand/01-logos/xr-guild-wordmark-stacked-cyan-on-indigo.svg. Clear space equals the height of the X on all four sides. Minimum 90px wide on screen for the stacked wordmark, 150px for the horizontal, 16px for the XR tile. Use the XR tile for square or small spaces. Never stretch, rotate, outline, recolour outside the palette, or fill the letterforms with texture or photography — the texture goes behind the mark. Never place another organisation's logo inside an XR Guild lockup.
Do: Say what it costs — it is free, and that is the most important fact on the page; Say beginners are welcome, in those words; Name the date, time, and venue so they are readable across a bar; Show people, not products; Carry all four parts of the invitation grammar: who this is, the invitation, where and when, and the no-barrier line; Use the real attendance number when quoting one; Describe membership plainly when it is the subject, in its own place — something to join, never something to be outside of
Avoid: Implying that attending an event requires membership, approval, or an invitation — event-facing work must never read as exclusive; Attaching membership to an event invitation, or phrasing membership as a tier, level, or upgrade; Jargon or acronyms in public copy; Urgency and scarcity language such as limited spots or do not miss out; Omitting the no-barrier line to save space; Placing a venue or partner logo inside an XR Guild lockup; Promising attendee data to a sponsor — attendees are guests, not leads; Advertising language on behalf of a sponsor: comparative claims, pricing, endorsements, or a call to buy. Acknowledgement only; Photographing people who did not agree to it
CTA style: Invite a next step and name what it costs, which is nothing. Never urgency, scarcity, or pressure. Example: Grab a free spot on Luma. Turn up, try a headset, leave whenever you like.

## Hook

Cities keep independently inventing the same thing.

## Caption

Five cities kept inventing the same thing without asking each other.

Boston, Seattle, Akron, New York and Toronto all run XR communities built around meeting in person rather than posting online. Boston and Seattle say the purpose outright: people come to demonstrate and to try VR, not to watch a talk about it.

Which makes sense given the price. A headset costs several hundred dollars just to find out whether you like it. A room where one already exists removes that question.

XR Guild runs one of these. A 501(c)3 non-profit, founded in 2022 and run entirely by volunteers, with demo nights open to anyone, free, and nothing to join.

There is likely one where you are too. Search your city and "XR" or "VR meetup". If nothing turns up, four people and one headset is a real start.

Grab a free spot on Luma. Turn up, try a headset, leave whenever you like.

Sourced from a web-search sweep of public posts across five community accounts. Nothing here was opened and verified beyond the search snippet, so treat the city list as what those communities say about themselves.

## Evidence summary

Across 27 captured X snippets, the clearest signal is structural rather than topical: independent regional XR communities in Boston, Seattle, Akron, New York and Toronto all organise around recurring in-person meetups whose stated purpose is letting people try hardware. That is the same format XR Guild already runs, which makes these peers rather than competitors. Newcomer-facing signal is thinner: first-contact posts read as social, and one community member coaches newcomers through motion sickness, but neither is a measured pattern. Digg produced no topical results and Reddit is unreachable from this host, so this is a single-platform sample and nothing here was verified against an opened source.
